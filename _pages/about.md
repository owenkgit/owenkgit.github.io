I graduated with an MS from Carnegie Mellon University, where I worked on robotics with Prof. Jean Oh and Prof. Amir Barati Farimani.

I started at the source of human intent: decoding motor imagery from EEG, asking how much of what someone wants can be read from brain activity alone. That led me to the body—predicting cardiac arrest hours ahead from the sparse vital signs a hospital actually records. Then to the machine that has to act on it: robot policies, and how to correct them at inference time from plain language, so a robot can be fixed mid-task by being told what it got wrong. Brain, body, machine—I want to build the whole chain in one system, a robot that reads human intent from whatever signal is available and adapts to it in the moment.

I also build in the open, contributing to [RIO](https://robot-i-o.github.io/) and [OpenBMI](https://github.com/PatternRecognition/OpenBMI), and releasing the experiments that worked, like [autosimulation](https://github.com/owenk3/autosimulation).
