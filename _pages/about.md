I graduated with an MS from Carnegie Mellon University, where I worked on robotics with Prof. Jean Oh and Prof. Amir Barati Farimani.

I started at the source of human intent: decoding motor imagery from EEG, asking how much of what someone wants can be read from brain activity alone. That led me to the body—predicting cardiac arrest hours ahead from the sparse vital signs a hospital actually records. Then to the machine that has to act on it: robot policies, and how to correct one while it runs, without retraining it. In CorrectVLA a single sentence repairs a frozen policy's motion; in RT-Cache a retrieved memory stands in for training; RIO is the open-source platform both run on. Brain, body, machine—what I want to build is a robot the people alongside it can correct, and to know which signals a person can supply, how few a policy needs, and when correction stops working.

I also build in the open, contributing to [RIO](https://robot-i-o.github.io/) and [OpenBMI](https://github.com/PatternRecognition/OpenBMI), and releasing the experiments that worked, like [autosimulation](https://github.com/owenk3/autosimulation).
