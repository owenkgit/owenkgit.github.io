I graduated with an MS from Carnegie Mellon University, focusing on robotics under the guidance of Prof. Jean Oh and Prof. Amir Barati Farimani.

I started at the source of human intent—decoding motor imagery straight from EEG, and asking how much of what someone wants can be read from brain activity alone. That led me to the body, predicting cardiac arrest hours ahead from the sparse vital signs a hospital actually records. Then to the machine that has to act on it: robot policies, and how to correct them at inference time from plain language, so a robot can be fixed mid-task by being told what it got wrong.

Brain, body, machine. I've been working my way along that chain, and what I want to build is the whole of it in one system—a robot that reads human intent from whatever signal is available and adapts to it in the moment.

I also like building things in the open—contributing to [RIO](https://robot-i-o.github.io/) and [OpenBMI](https://github.com/PatternRecognition/OpenBMI), and releasing experiments that worked, like [autosimulation](https://github.com/owenk3/autosimulation).
